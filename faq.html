Frequently Asked Questions

What is unclaimed money?

Unclaimed money (also called unclaimed property) is any financial asset that has been abandoned or forgotten by its rightful owner. This includes forgotten bank accounts, uncashed checks, insurance payments, security deposits, stocks, bonds, and more. When these assets remain unclaimed for a certain period (typically 1-5 years), they are turned over to the state for safekeeping.

Is this service really free?

Yes, absolutely! Unclaimed Money Finder is completely free to use. We never charge for searching our database or for providing claim instructions. Beware of services that charge fees or ask for a percentage of your claim - you can always claim your money directly through your state's unclaimed property office for free.

How do I know if the money is really mine?

Our search results provide detailed information including the source of the funds, the company or institution holding them, the approximate year they were reported, and the location. You'll need to verify your identity when filing a claim with the appropriate agency. If you're unsure whether a particular result belongs to you, you can contact the holding institution for more information.

How long does it take to receive my money?

The time frame varies by state and the complexity of your claim. Simple claims with proper documentation typically take 4-8 weeks to process. More complex claims involving estates, businesses, or missing documentation may take several months. Each state has its own processing times.

What documents do I need to claim my money?

Generally, you'll need:

  • Proof of identity (driver's license, passport, or state ID)
  • Social Security number
  • Proof of address (utility bill, bank statement)
  • Documentation connecting you to the unclaimed property (if available)
  • Additional documents may be required depending on the type and amount of the claim

Is there a time limit to claim my money?

Good news! In most states, there is no time limit to claim unclaimed property. The state will hold your money indefinitely until you claim it. However, it's best to claim it as soon as possible to avoid any complications.

Can I search for family members or deceased relatives?

Yes! You can search for anyone's name. If you find unclaimed property belonging to a deceased relative, you may be able to claim it as an heir. You'll typically need to provide a death certificate, proof of your relationship, and possibly additional estate documents.

Why didn't the company contact me directly?

Companies are required to attempt to contact owners before turning property over to the state. However, these attempts often fail because:

  • You moved and didn't update your address
  • Mail was returned as undeliverable
  • Email addresses changed
  • Phone numbers were disconnected
  • Name changes due to marriage or divorce

How do I actually claim the money once I find it?

The claiming process typically involves:

  1. Clicking the "How to Claim This Money" button on your search result
  2. Visiting your state's unclaimed property website
  3. Filing a claim form (usually available online)
  4. Submitting required documentation
  5. Waiting for your claim to be processed
  6. Receiving your payment (usually by check)

Are there any scams I should watch out for?

Unfortunately, yes. Be cautious of:

  • Anyone charging upfront fees to search or claim
  • Requests for payment information before you receive your money
  • Pressure tactics or urgent demands
  • Requests for unusual personal information
  • Unsolicited phone calls or emails about unclaimed money

Remember: legitimate unclaimed property programs are always free. Only work with official state agencies or verified services like ours.

What if I don't find anything?

If your search doesn't return results, don't give up! Try:

  • Searching with name variations (maiden names, middle names)
  • Searching previous addresses or states where you've lived
  • Searching for family members who might have listed you as a beneficiary
  • Checking back periodically - new properties are reported regularly

How often is your database updated?

We update our database regularly with information from state unclaimed property offices and other sources. However, there may be a delay between when property is turned over to the state and when it appears in our database. For the most current information, you can also check your state's official unclaimed property website.

What types of unclaimed property are included?

Our database includes many types of unclaimed property:

  • Bank accounts and safe deposit box contents
  • Uncashed checks (paychecks, refunds, rebates)
  • Insurance policy proceeds and refunds
  • Stocks, bonds, and mutual funds
  • Utility deposits and refunds
  • Security deposits
  • Court deposits
  • Unclaimed wages or commissions
  • Mineral royalty payments
